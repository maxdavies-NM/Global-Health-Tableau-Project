# Tableau Project - Global Health Survey

This repository contains an interactive Tableau dashboard that was created during my Data Technician bootcamp. The dashboard was created to analyse global health survey data, and explores patterns in health indicators across countries.
This project demonstrates my ability to create and design Tableau dashboards, to analyse real-world datasets, and to communicate discovories through clear storytelling.

## Skills Used

### Tableau Data Visualisation
- Choropleth mapping for geographic analysis  
- Bar charts for categorical comparisons  
- Line charts for time‑series trends  
- Grouped bar charts for demographic breakdowns  
- Dashboard layout design and interactive filtering  

### Analytical Skills
- Identifying patterns in life expectancy, BMI, and population growth  
- Comparing health indicators across continents and countries
- Interpreting relationships between variables (e.g., BMI vs. life expectancy)  
- Summarising insights clearly and concisely
- Creating calculated fields for deeper insights

### Storytelling & Presentation
- Designing a clear narrative around global health trends  
- Using colour, layout, and chart selection to guide interpretation  
- Writing accessible explanations for non‑technical audiences  

### Technical Skills
- Tableau Public publishing and dashboard optimisation  
- GitHub repository structuring and documentation  
- Markdown formatting and README creation

### What I Found

> "Europe has the highest life expectancy</span>There are higher average life expectancy rates across Europe, America, Oceania, and some parts of Asia. Whereas lower life expectancy mostly appear in regions of Africa and some parts of Asia. 
For total cancer rates we can see that China has a very high total amount of cancer with an amount of 21,000,000. This exceeds other countries massively. Although nowhere near China the US and Japan also have high cancer rates of around 4,000,000. It is also seen that the countries with the highest total amount of cancer are all from Asia, America, and Europe.
From the BMI chart we can see that for both men and women Oceania has the highest with scores of 26.744 and 27.868. Americas and Europe both have high BMI levels whereas Africa and Asisa have low levels.
From the Population growth we can see that Africa has the population growth from 1990-2008. Europe and Oceania have a slow but steady population rate. Asia showed a large dip from about 1992 to 2005. The Americas have a steady population growth rate."
